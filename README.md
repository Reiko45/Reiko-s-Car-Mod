# Reiko's Car Mod

Drivable cars, motorcycles, semi trucks, tow trucks, a mail van and work vehicles (a police car, an ambulance, a
garbage truck, an ice cream truck, a dump truck and a cement mixer) for Minecraft 1.21.1 (NeoForge) that run on
**BuildCraft Reborn**'s fuel, trailers for the semis to pull, and gas and diesel pumps that sell the fuel for **cash or
card** through **Reiko's Economy Mod**.

Refine oil into fuel with BuildCraft, pipe it into a pump, set a price, and other players pay you to fill up.
Cars wear out as they're driven and show where they've crashed, and a **Repair Bench** fixes them: fit your own parts,
or run a mechanic's shop and charge for repairs. Tow trucks haul broken-down cars to it, and mailboxes and the mail van carry parcels between
players for postage. Parking meters ticket cars that overstay, toll gates lift for drivers who pay, insurance offices
cover crash repairs, and the job board pays drivers, mail carriers, mechanics, truckers and garbage collectors.
Every vehicle has turn signals and hazard lights, and a locked car's alarm catches anyone without a key. Lay
**asphalt** roads and paint them, and put up **traffic lights**, signs and street lamps; red-light and speed cameras
fine drivers for their owners. And every car has a **radio**: three stations of original music, or your own discs.

There are **electric cars** too, charged at **charging stations** that BuildCraft engines and kinesis pipes power, and
that sell the charge for cash or card the same way. And a **fire truck**: a fire hose that sprays from its water
tank, a ladder that raises up the back, and **fire hydrants** to fill it from BuildCraft pumps.

Dealerships sell cars **on finance**, paid off a day at a time from the bank, and a car whose payments stop is
**repossessed** to an **impound lot**, where tow trucks also bring cars left at expired parking meters. **Rental
desks** hire out cars by the day; a rented car goes back to its lot by itself when the time's up.

Every car carries a **license plate**, and drivers earn a **driver's license** by passing a driving test at a **DMV**,
round its owner's numbered traffic cones. Cameras put penalty points on it, enough points suspend it, and police
officers with a **Ticket Book** check plates and fine unlicensed drivers.

A **Tuning Bench** fits engine stages, turbos, tires, brakes and suspension kits, and paints race numbers and stripes;
a **Race Control** and numbered **race flags** make a race track, with timed laps, a best-lap board, and races for a
pot of entry fees.

Rain, snow and **thunderstorms** make the roads slippery: **windshield wipers** sweep the rain off, spray flies off the
tires, snow piles up on parked cars, lightning strikes now and then, and **Winter Tires** grip on snow and ice.

Cars get **dirty** off-road, in mud and in the rain, and it shows. A drive-through **Car Wash** with spinning
brushes, a coin-op **Wash Bay** with a pressure washer, or a bucket of water gets them clean again; left filthy, a car
rusts and dealers pay less for it.

A **GPS** (**N**, in a car or on foot) finds the nearest gas station, mechanic or car wash, with their prices, and
leads you back to where you parked. With **Xaero's Minimap**, its destination and your cars show on the minimap too.

**Bus stops** (a sign on a pole, or a glass shelter with a bench to sit on) make bus routes: the city bus calls at its
owner's stops in order, opens its doors for whoever's waiting, lets off whoever rang the bell, carries villagers between
stops for a fare, and the job board pays its driver for each stop served.

**Campers** hitch to a pickup's tow ball (or the semi's fifth wheel): sleep in one to skip the night and set your spawn,
stock its cupboards, cook on its stove, and when it's parked its awning, steps and camp chairs fold out. **Livestock
trailers** carry cows, sheep, pigs, goats, horses, llamas and chickens: lead them up the ramp and watch them ride
between the slats.

**Garages:** build a **garage door** any size from panels (a house's sectional door that rolls up under the ceiling,
or a metal **roller shutter**), paint it, and open it by hand, with a **remote**, a wall button, or just by driving up
to it. A car shut in a garage can't be stolen, towed or impounded. Or run it as a **parking garage**, charging drivers
by the hour or the day.

A **Car Manual** (with Patchouli installed) explains all of it in game, with every vehicle turning in 3D, its recipes
and pictures: every player gets one the first time they join.

## The Car Manual

The mod's guide book, shown by **Patchouli**. It has a chapter for each part of the mod: getting started, driving,
every vehicle (each turning in 3D, with its seats, top speed, tank and recipes), fuel and charging, repairs and
washing, towing, roads, garages, money and business, public services, and tuning and racing, with screenshots of each.

- **Getting one:** every player is given one the first time they join a world (once: not again after dying or
  losing it). Make another from a **book** and an **iron ingot**, or open it from the **Guide** button at the top of
  the Car Workbench (that opens it at the page on building cars).
- **Searching:** Patchouli's search box, on the book's front page, finds any entry.
- **Keys:** the book shows each key as you have it set (the hitch key as **G**, unless you've changed it).
- **Without Patchouli** there's no book, no recipe and no Guide button; everything else works the same.

Tank sizes are in buckets of the car's own fuel: BuildCraft's **light fuel** for the gasoline cars, **dense fuel**
for the diesel ones (the semi, delivery van, city bus, heavy-duty pickup, farm tractor, tow trucks and the big work
vehicles). A sedan goes about 600
blocks on a bucket; a semi about 250, or 180 pulling a trailer; the bus about 300. The electric cars' batteries are in
BuildCraft MJ: a full charge takes the City EV about 2,500 blocks, the Electric Sedan about 3,500.

**Driving:** right-click a car to get in (the first person in drives), with one of its **keys** in your inventory
(see [Car keys](#car-keys)). The movement keys drive and steer, **jump**
is the handbrake and **sneak** gets out. **H** sounds the horn, **L** locks or unlocks the car and **G** hitches a
trailer, loads a car onto a car hauler, picks up or drops a car in a tow truck, stops the mail van for the mail, or
does a work vehicle's job. **K** works a police car's, ambulance's or fire truck's lights and siren. The **left and right arrow
keys** work the turn signals and the **down arrow** the hazards (see [Turn signals, hazards and alarms](#turn-signals-hazards-and-alarms)).
**R** opens the [radio](#car-radio) and **N** the [GPS](#gps). In a city bus, **G** opens the doors at a
[bus stop](#bus-stops), and its passengers press **G** to ring for the next stop. While you're
in a car, a dashboard in the bottom right shows your speed and a fuel gauge. (If the screen's too narrow for it to
fit beside the hotbar, at a big GUI scale, it moves to the top right, under your status effects.)

- **Fuel:** cars burn fuel for every block they cover, and a little while idling with someone at the wheel. The cars
  run on **gasoline**: BuildCraft's Light Fuel (and the Mixed Light and Gaseous Fuels), or other mods' gasoline,
  ethanol, kerosene and LPG. The semi, delivery van, city bus, heavy-duty pickup, farm tractor, tow trucks, ambulance,
  garbage truck, dump truck, cement mixer and fire truck run on **diesel**:
  BuildCraft's Dense Fuel and Mixed Heavy Fuels, or other mods' diesel and biodiesel. Neither takes the other's fuel, and nothing runs on crude oil. How far a bucket goes
  comes from BuildCraft's own fuel values. The `reikocars:gasoline` and `reikocars:diesel` fluid tags decide which
  fuel is which, so a data pack can move fluids between them. A car's tooltip says what it runs on.
- **Filling up:** buy gasoline at a gas pump and diesel at a diesel pump, or (if the server allows it) pour a bucket
  of the right fuel straight into the tank.
- **Ownership:** whoever puts a car down owns it. New cars start **locked**, so only the owner and anyone with its
  key can get in, though anyone can ride along with them. Only the owner (or an admin) can pick a car back up. Punch
  it a few times and it drops as an item, keeping its fuel, paint and locks. A pickup's bed spills its items.
- **Paint:** right-click a car with any dye.
- **Headlights:** the lights come on with the engine, and in the dark (at night, in a tunnel or a cave) they really
  light the road: brightly about twelve blocks ahead and softly just in front, moving along as you drive. The light
  stops at walls, so it doesn't shine through into buildings. It's real block light (from invisible light blocks
  that follow the car and tidy themselves away), so it works with any shaders and mobs won't spawn where it falls. A
  server can turn it off with `driving.realHeadlights`, leaving the lights to just glow.
- **Off-road:** grass, dirt, sand, snow and the like (the `reikocars:off_road` block tag) slow every car but the
  off-roader, the heavy-duty pickup, the farm tractor, the dump truck, the electric pickup and the dirt bike. Ice is
  slippery.
- **Running things over** hurts mobs and knocks them aside, more the faster you go. Players only get hurt with PvP
  on, and never your own pets.
- Cars climb a full block, so curbs, slabs and stairs don't stop them. Cars can't be blown up or burned.

## Car keys

Every car, motorcycle and truck needs one of its keys to start, **even for its owner**. With no key in your inventory
the engine won't turn over and the dashboard says **NO KEY**.

- **Cutting keys:** craft **Blank Keys** (an iron ingot and a gold nugget make two), then **sneak + right-click**
  your car with one to cut a key for it. Cut as many as you like and hand them out. Only the owner (or an admin) can
  cut keys. A key is named for its car ("Sedan Key"), its fob is in the car's paint, and its tooltip names the owner.
- **What a key does:** anyone carrying it can start and drive the car, get into it while it's locked, open its trunk
  or bed, and lock or unlock it with **L**. They can't pick it up or cut more keys.
- **Key fob:** right-click with a key to lock or unlock its car from up to 32 blocks away. The lights flash.
- **Lost a key?** Changing the locks makes every other key stop fitting: the owner **sneak + right-clicks** the car
  twice with one of its keys, and only that key fits from then on. (Lost every key? Cut a new one from a blank first.)
- Picking a car up keeps its locks, so its keys still fit when you put it down again.
- **Trailers** take keys too, cut the same way: a key lets someone open, hitch and lock a locked trailer.

A server can set `driving.requireKeys` to `false`, so cars start without keys and keys only share locked cars.
Cars from before 1.12.0 have no keys yet: cut one before driving.

## Turn signals, hazards and alarms

- **Turn signals:** the driver's **left arrow** and **right arrow** signal left and right (press again to cancel).
  That side's indicators, taillights, mirror repeaters and side markers blink amber, an arrow blinks on the
  dashboard, and the relay ticks. A signal cancels itself once the car has turned at least 40° that way and is
  running straight again, so a lane change doesn't cancel it but a corner does. A semi's trailer blinks with it. Motorcycles have indicators on little stalks either side of their headlight and taillight.
- **Hazards:** the **down arrow** blinks both sides until it's pressed again. A car that **breaks down** puts its
  hazards on by itself.
- **Car alarm:** a **locked** car's alarm goes off when someone without its key hits it, tries to get in or tries to
  open its trunk. It blares for half a minute with every light flashing, and its owner (if they're on) gets a chat
  message saying where it is and who set it off. The owner or anyone with its key stops it with **L**, with the
  key's lock button (right-click with the key; the car stays locked), or by getting in. Unlocked cars have no alarm.
  A server can change how long alarms sound, or turn them off, with `driving.alarmSeconds`.

All the keys can be changed under Controls, in the Reiko's Car Mod section.

## Car radio

Every car, truck and bike has a radio. Anyone riding presses **R** to open it:

- **Power**, and three **station presets**, each playing its own tracks one after another, round the clock:

  | Station | Plays | Tracks |
  | --- | --- | --- |
  | **88.1 Neon FM** | synthwave | Neon Highway, Midnight Drive |
  | **94.5 Chill FM** | lo-fi beats | Rainy Window, Late Bus Home |
  | **101.3 Dusty Roads** | country guitar | Gravel Road, Dust on the Dash |

  The music was composed and synthesised for this mod. The stations are on the air whether anyone's listening or
  not, so tuning in drops you into the middle of whatever's on, and every car on the same station plays the same
  moment of the same song.
- **DISC** plays whatever's in the radio's **disc slot**, from the top when it goes in (or the radio's switched to
  it, or someone gets into the empty car), then again after a three-second pause, like a jukebox: any music disc,
  vanilla or another mod's. Shift-click a disc in from your
  inventory.
- **Volume** from 0 to 10, shown on the display along with what's playing. A new track shows **Now Playing** over
  the hotbar.

The radio plays while someone's in the car, and keeps its station, volume and disc when everyone gets out. Picking
the car up (or breaking it) drops the disc, and the radio starts off again when it's put back down. Riders hear it at
its volume; people nearby hear a quarter of that, or half from a convertible, tractor or bike. Someone who comes
into earshot, or joins a car that's already playing, hears the song from wherever it's got to, not from the start. It plays on the
**Jukebox/Note Blocks** volume slider.

## Roads

- **Asphalt** (block, slab and stairs) makes a road: cars go **15% faster** on it, and their tires wear a quarter
  slower. Craft it from gravel and coal.
- **Road Paint** marks it. Right-click the air to pick a marking (sneak to go back): a dashed or double yellow center
  line, a dashed white lane line, an edge line, a stop line, a crosswalk, or an arrow (straight on, left or right).
  Then right-click the top of asphalt or an asphalt slab: the marking is drawn the way you're facing (arrows point
  that way, an edge line runs down your left, a stop line lies across the far side). Paint the same thing again to
  scrape it off. A can paints 256 blocks.
- **Street Poles** (stack as many as you like) hold up everything else: traffic lights, signs and street lamps all go
  on top of one, facing whoever puts them up (a lamp's arm reaches out the way you face).
- **Street Lamps** come on at dusk and go off at dawn.
- **Stop Signs** are just signs; paint a stop line by them.

### Traffic lights and cameras

- **Traffic lights** run on one shared clock: 10 seconds of green, 3 of yellow, then red. Lights facing north or
  south take turns with lights facing east or west, with a second of red all round in between, so put one up facing
  each road into a crossing and they work together. A **redstone** signal holds a light on red.
- **Red-light cameras:** a light's owner (whoever put it up) can fine drivers who run its red: a car that crosses the
  line through the light, heading toward its face, within 8 blocks across the road, while it's red. Right-click your
  light to set the fine ($15.00 to start; 0 turns the camera off) and see the tickets it's written. Anyone else
  right-clicking it is told the fine.
- **Speed limit signs** show their limit. Their owner right-clicks one to set the limit (mph or km/h) and the speed
  camera's fine ($20.00 to start; 0 for none). A car going more than 2 mph (3 km/h) over the limit within 12 blocks
  of the sign is fined.
- **Fines** come out of the car owner's bank account (whatever they have, if it's less than the fine) and go to the
  camera's owner; both get a chat message, and the camera flashes. A car is fined at most once a minute by each kind
  of camera. The camera owner's own cars, cars with nobody driving, and police cars and ambulances with their sirens
  on aren't fined. Only the owner (or an admin) can break a traffic light or speed sign.
- **On the driver's license:** a camera's fine also puts penalty points on the license of whoever was driving (3 for
  a red light, 2 for speeding), and a driver with no valid license pays a $25 fine on top, to the camera's owner (see
  [License plates and driver's licenses](#license-plates-and-drivers-licenses)).

## License plates and driver's licenses

**Plates.** Every car and motorcycle gets a license plate the first time it's in the world: three letters and three
digits ("RKC 482"), different from every other car's, written on the plate panels on its back (and front, on cars
that have one; a motorcycle's small plate takes it on two lines). It stays with the car when it's picked up and put
down, and shows in its tooltip, on tickets and fines ("Sedan (RKC 482)") and in police plate checks.

**The DMV.** Put down a **DMV Office** (a counter under a DMV sign, with a license camera and an eye chart). Whoever
places it owns it, and only they (or an admin) can break it. Right-click it:

- **License tab:** your license, its number and its points. Without one, pay for the **driving test** ($25.00 to start,
  plus sales tax, by card or cash); with one, buy a **new card** ($10.00) if you've lost yours.
- **Plates tab:** pick one of your cars parked within **10 blocks** and type a **custom plate** of 2 to 8 letters,
  digits and spaces ($100.00). Nobody else can have the same plate; the old one is freed.
- **Settings tab** (the owner): the three fees, the test route, and the books: tests taken and passed, plates sold and
  what it's all earned. The owner pays nothing at their own DMV.

**The driving test.** The route is the DMV owner's **numbered traffic cones** within 128 blocks of it. Put down
**Traffic Cones** (cars and people go through them) and right-click each to number it 1, 2, 3... (sneak to count
down; 0 takes it off the route). Numbered cones show their number, and only their owner can pick them up. Having paid,
get into any car you may drive and go through every cone in order (within 3½ blocks of each) inside **5 minutes**.
Sparkles over the next cone show the way, and the action bar shows which cone is next and the time left. Crashing (any
body damage), running a red light, passing a speed limit sign too fast, getting out or into another car, or running
out of time fails it; pay again to retry. Pass, and the license is yours, with a **Driver's License** card.

**Points and suspension.** Red-light and speed cameras add penalty points to the driver's license (3 and 2). Points
drop off after 7 Minecraft days; 12 at once **suspends** the license for 3 days and wipes them. The card shows its
points or suspension, and right-clicking it checks it against the records. A lost card is only a card: the license is
the record, and any DMV prints a new one.

**Without a valid license** (none, or suspended):

- a camera that catches you adds a **$25** fine on top, to its owner;
- a **police officer** can ticket you: someone in the **Police Officer** job (or an admin) holding a **Ticket Book**
  right-clicks a car to see its plate, its owner and who's driving with their license, or right-clicks a player to
  see theirs. An unlicensed driver is fined $25 from their bank account (at most once every ten minutes); the fine
  goes to nobody, and the ticket pays the officer's job;
- and if the server sets `licenses.required`, you can't drive at all: the car won't start and the dashboard says
  **NO LICENSE**. The driving test is the exception: you can drive on it.

## Fares

The **city bus** and the **taxi** take fares from their passengers, paid to whoever's driving. The vehicle's owner
rides free.

- **The bus:** sit down while someone's driving and a pay screen opens: the fare (plus sales tax), by **Card** with
  PIN or **Cash**. Close it or press **Get off** and you're off the bus. Each time you get on is a new ride.
- **The taxi:** get in while someone's driving and you've hired it. The **meter** starts at a starting fare and adds
  so much for every 100 blocks driven; the dashboard shows it (**FARE $3.40**). When you get out, the fare comes out
  of your **bank account** and goes to the driver. Get out within 5 blocks and it's free. If you can't cover it all,
  you pay what you have. Other passengers ride along on the same hire.
- **Setting fares:** the owner **sneak + right-clicks** the bus or taxi with an empty hand: a fare per ride for the
  bus, or a starting fare and a rate per 100 blocks for the taxi, and what it's earned so far. 0 makes rides free.
  Anyone else doing that is told the fares.

New buses charge $2.00 a ride, and new taxis $3.00 plus $1.00 per 100 blocks (see [Settings](#settings)).

## Bus stops

A **Bus Stop Sign** (a blue BUS STOP plate on a pole) or a **Bus Shelter** (three blocks wide and two high: a glass
back and ends, a roof with a sign along its front, a lamp, a route map, and a bench) makes a stop. Whoever puts it
down owns it, and only they (or an admin) can break it; breaking any block of a shelter takes it all down.

- **Naming and routes:** the owner right-clicks the stop to name it (up to 24 letters) and put it on up to three
  **routes** (1 to 99), with its **stop number** along each: 1 for the first stop, 2 for the next, and so on. The
  name is written on the sign's strip and along the shelter's roof, with the routes that stop there.
- **The bus's route:** the bus's owner sneaks and right-clicks it with an empty hand, where the fare is set, and gives
  it a route number. A bus on route 1 calls at **its owner's** route-1 stops, in number order, round and round. The
  dashboard shows the route and the next stop (**Route 1 · Next: Oak Ave**) to the driver and everyone aboard.
- **Waiting:** right-click a stop to wait there (for five minutes, within six blocks of it), or right-click a shelter's
  bench to sit down and wait. Owners sneak to do it at their own stops.
- **The doors:** a bus driver who's stopped by a stop (within 8 blocks) presses **G** (the dashboard shows
  **[G] Open doors**). The doors open with a hiss: passengers who rang get off, everyone waiting there gets on (and
  pays the fare as usual), and everyone aboard hears the chime and is told the stop and the next one. Getting on this
  way doesn't need the bus's key.
- **The bell:** a passenger presses **G** to ring for the next stop: a ding, and **STOP REQUESTED** on the dashboard
  until the driver opens the doors.
- **Villagers:** villagers standing by a stop on the bus's route get on too (each has an even chance, at most three a
  stop), for another stop on the route at least 32 blocks away. When the doors open at their stop they get off and
  pay the driver $1.00 (new money, from nobody's account). A villager rests for half a day before riding again, and
  one who's ridden ten minutes without getting there gets off at the next stop.
- **Bus Driver pay:** the Driver job pays for each stop served in order along a route ($0.75), when it's at least 24
  blocks from the last one and the bus has driven most of the way there, even with nobody aboard; and for each
  villager who rides to their stop, as for a paying passenger.
- **On the GPS:** stops show in the GPS's Places as **Bus stop · Oak Ave**, with their routes.

## Buying and selling cars

Any car can change hands, with its keys: the buyer gets a key cut for **new locks**, so the seller's keys stop fitting.

- **For Sale Signs:** right-click your own car with a For Sale Sign (it isn't used up) to put it up for sale at a
  price, change the price, or take it off sale. A **FOR SALE** sign with the price floats over it.
- **Buying:** right-click someone else's car that's for sale. The screen shows its condition, fuel, seller and price;
  pay by **Card** with PIN or **Cash**, plus sales tax, and the money goes to the seller. Nobody can be in it.

### Car dealerships

Put down a **Car Dealership** (a sales desk under a CARS sign). Whoever places it owns it, and only they (or an
admin) can break it. The cars its owner parks within **10 blocks** make up the lot.

- **The owner's Lot tab:** pick each of your cars on the lot with the arrows, set its price or take it off sale.
- **The owner's Trade-ins tab:** what the dealership pays for each kind of car (pick the kind with the arrows). That's
  for one in perfect condition; worn ones fetch less, down to 40% for a wreck. 0 means it isn't taking that kind.
  The tab also shows the books: cars sold and trade-ins bought.
- **Customers' Buy tab:** browse the cars for sale on the lot, then **Buy this car...** for the paying screen (or
  just right-click the car).
- **Customers' Trade in tab:** park your own car on the lot and sell it to the dealership for its offer, paid from the
  dealer's bank account. The car becomes the dealer's, ready for them to price.

### Car loans

A car for sale on a dealership's lot can be bought **on finance**. Its buy screen has **Pay in full** and
**Finance** buttons; on finance you pay **20% down** now (plus the sales tax on the whole price, by card or cash),
and the rest, with **10% interest** on top, comes out of your bank account in **10 daily payments** (one every
Minecraft day, 20 minutes) to the dealer. A $1,500 car is $300 down, then $132 a day. The car is yours from the start,
with a key for new locks.

- **Until it's paid off** it can't be put up for sale, traded in or picked up. The dealership's **Trade in** tab
  shows what's still owed on it, and **Pay off the loan** pays the rest from your bank account at once.
- **Missed payments:** a payment your bank account can't cover is missed (you get a message each time). After
  **3 missed in a row**, the car is **repossessed**: it's taken away, wherever it is, to the impound lot nearest the
  dealership (see [Impound lots](#impound-lots)). Pay the missed payments and the lot's fee there within **5 days**
  and it's yours again, with the loan back on track; the missed payments go to the dealer. If you don't, the car
  is the dealer's, on new locks, and the loan's done. With no impound lot anywhere, the dealer takes it straight back.

## Impound lots

Put down an **Impound Office** (a booth under a yellow IMPOUND sign). Whoever places it owns it, and only they (or an
admin) can break it. Its lot is everything within **10 blocks**. Fence it in if you like.

- **Towing in:** a car left at an **expired parking meter** (its time run out, or never paid, past the grace minute,
  at a meter that isn't its owner's) can be picked up by either tow truck even though it's locked and not yours.
  Put it down on an impound lot and it's **impounded**. The tow pays the Mechanic job (unless it's your own car or
  your own lot), and the car's owner gets a message saying where it is and what the fee is.
- **Repossessed cars** from missed loan payments are brought there too.
- **While it's held** an **IMPOUNDED** sign floats over it, and its owner can't get in, drive it, open it or pick it
  up. The lot's owner can move it about.
- **Getting it back:** right-click the office. It shows your cars held there, why, for how long and what it costs:
  the **release fee** (plus sales tax), and for a repossessed car the missed loan payments as well. Pay by card or
  cash and it's yours again where it stands. The fee goes to the lot's owner.
- **The owner's side:** the owner's screen shows every car held there. They can **let a towed car go** for free
  (not a repossessed one: the dealer's owed), set the release fee, and see the books: cars towed in, released and
  fees taken. Breaking the office lets every car held there go.

## Car rentals

Put down a **Car Rental Desk** (a counter under a RENTALS sign) and park your cars within **10 blocks** of it: they
make up its fleet, unless they're up for sale or someone's in them.

- **Renting:** right-click the desk. Pick a car with the arrows, choose **1 to 7 days** (a day is 20 minutes), and
  pay the day rate times the days, plus sales tax, by card or cash. The money goes to the desk's owner.
- **While it's rented** the renter can open and drive it without a key; nobody else can. The dashboard shows the
  time left where it would say LOCKED, and you get a warning two minutes and thirty seconds before it's up.
- **Bringing it back early:** park it back on the lot and get out, and the rental ends.
- **When the time's up** it won't start for the renter any more. As soon as nobody's in it, it goes back to its spot
  on the lot by itself, wherever it was left (even far away, in chunks nobody's in).
- **The owner's side:** the **Fleet** tab shows the cars on the lot and the ones out on rent, with the time each has
  left. The **Prices** tab sets a day's rent for each kind of car (0 stops renting that kind out), and both show the
  books: rentals and takings. A rented car can't be put up for sale until it's back.

## Tuning

Put down a **Tuning Bench** (a dark workbench with a turbo and a dyno screen, under a TUNING sign). Whoever places it
owns it, and only they (or an admin) can break it. Park a car within **6 blocks** and right-click the bench. Only a
car's owner can have it tuned.

| Kit | What it does | What it costs the car |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Engine Tune Kit | One engine stage (up to 3): +6% top speed and +4% pull a stage | 8% more fuel and 15% more engine wear a stage |
| Turbocharger | +25% acceleration | 10% more fuel and engine wear |
| Sport Tires | Turns 15% sharper | Slower off-road, and they wear 30% faster |
| Off-Road Tires | Far less slowed on grass, sand and snow | 3% off the top speed |
| Performance Brakes | One stage (up to 2): stops 20% harder a stage | Nothing |
| Lowering Springs | Turns 10% sharper | Slower off-road |
| Lift Kit | Less slowed off-road | Turns 5% wider |
| Extended Fuel Tank | One tank stage (up to 2): the tank holds 25% more a stage, so a sedan's 8 buckets become 10, then 12 | The weight takes 3% off acceleration a stage; gasoline and diesel cars only |
| Extra Battery Module | One battery stage (up to 2): the battery holds 25% more a stage, so it goes further on a charge | The weight takes 3% off acceleration a stage; electric cars only |
| Winter Tires | Far better grip on snow and ice, and better in the rain (see [Weather](#weather)) | 2% off the top speed, and they wear 20% faster |

- **Tune tab:** each kit, how far the car has it, a **Fit** button that fits one of your own kits for nothing, and a
  button with the bench's price for one from its stock. Pick several and pay the bill by card or cash (plus sales
  tax); the money goes to the bench's owner, whose own jobs are free. One set of tires and one set of springs: fitting
  another kind hands the old ones back.
- **Livery tab:** a **race number** (1 to 99) on a white roundel on both doors, and **racing stripes** in any dye color
  along the hood, roof and trunk. Free. Motorcycles don't take a livery.
- **The owner's Stock and Prices tabs:** load the bench with kits (hoppers and pipes can fill it too) and set what
  fitting each costs ($40.00 to start). Fitting kits to someone else's car pays the Mechanic job.

A car keeps its tuning and livery when it's picked up (and the fuel or charge in a bigger tank or battery); its
tooltip lists them. The bench only shows the kits a car can take: a fuel tank for gasoline and diesel cars, a battery
module for electric ones.

## Racing

Put down a **Race Control** (a booth with start lights on a checkered gantry): driving past it, within 8 blocks, is
crossing the start and finish line. Then put down **Race Flags** and right-click each to number it 1, 2, 3... (sneak
to count down). The track is the Race Control's owner's numbered flags within 256 blocks: a lap is every flag in order
(passing within 5 blocks of each) and back over the line. Numbered flags show their number, and only their owner can
pick them up. Cars and people go through flags.

- **Time trials** are free: right-click the Race Control, pick **Time trial**, get into a car and cross the line to start
  the clock. Every lap is timed; getting out ends it.
- **Races:** right-click the Race Control and **enter**, paying the entry fee ($10.00 to start) into the pot. Once two
  are in, an entrant presses **Start the race** (the owner can start it any time). Everyone who's in a car within 24
  blocks of the line races (anyone who isn't gets their fee back); the start lights count down three seconds. First
  over the line after the last lap wins the pot, less the track's cut (10% to start); the rest have a minute to finish.
  Getting out puts you out. Up to 12 can race; if nobody finishes within 10 minutes, everyone gets their fee back.
- **The stopwatch:** racing or on a time trial, the top of the screen shows the clock, your lap, the next checkpoint
  and (racing) your place.
- **Best laps:** the fastest laps driven at a Race Control, each driver's best in each kind of car, go on its board
  (the top 10).
- **The owner's Settings tab:** laps (1 to 10), the entry fee, the track's cut, and the books: races, time trials and
  the track's takings. They can call a race off, and everyone gets their fee back.

Races aren't saved: a restart calls them off, with the fees refunded.

## Motorcycles

Four motorcycles ride like the cars (same keys, fuel, locks, paint and pumps), on gasoline:

- **Sport Bike**: the fastest thing in the mod and the quickest off the line, with a seat for a passenger.
- **Cruiser**: long, low and loud, with a passenger seat and **saddlebags**: sneak + use opens their 9 slots.
- **Dirt Bike**: one seat, high and light, and it keeps its speed on grass, sand and snow like the off-roader.
- **Scooter**: one seat, slow, with a tiny tank that lasts ages (about 2,500 blocks a bucket).

They turn tighter than cars and **lean into turns**, more the faster you go, and the rider and passenger lean with
them. In first person the view tilts a little too. Parked with nobody on, a bike stands over on its kickstand.

They're built at the Car Workbench like the cars, from their own **frame**, **bodywork** and **seat**, a **Motorcycle
Engine** and **two** tires: the front wheel goes in the front left slot and the back wheel in the rear left one.

## Semis and trailers

The **Semi Truck** pulls one trailer at a time. There are three (and a camper and a livestock trailer, in
[Campers and livestock trailers](#campers-and-livestock-trailers)):

| Trailer | Carries |
| --- | --- |
| **Box Trailer** | 54 slots of cargo. Right-click it to open it, like a double chest |
| **Tanker Trailer** | 64 buckets of any one fluid: fuel, oil, water, lava, other mods' fluids |
| **Car Hauler** | 2 cars, one behind the other |

**Building a trailer:** trailers are put together at the Car Workbench too, from the **Trailer Chassis** (which
every trailer takes), the trailer's own body (the **Box Trailer Body**, **Tanker Trailer Tank** or **Car Hauler
Deck**) and four **Tires**. They take no engine or interior, so those two slots are shaded for them.

**Putting a trailer down:** use the trailer item on the ground. It stands on its landing legs facing the way you
look, so face the way you'll drive off. Whoever puts it down owns it.

**Hitching:** back the semi up under the trailer's front, so the fifth wheel (the plate behind the cab) is under
the trailer's nose and the two are roughly in line (within 45°), then press **G**. The dashboard shows
**[G] Hitch trailer** when you're close enough. Press G again to drop it off where it stands.

**Driving a rig:** the trailer swings round behind you on bends and cuts the corner, as a real one does, and it can't
fold back on itself past 100°. The semi turns about its back wheels, so take corners wide. Pulling a trailer, the
semi is a little slower to get going and burns 40% more fuel. In third person the camera stands far enough back to
see the whole rig.

**Trailers are solid:** you can't walk through one, and cars bump into them as they do into each other. The rig
stops if the trailer would swing into a wall or a building (curbs, a block high, don't stop it). An unhitched
trailer's front, standing on its legs, is open underneath, so a semi can still back up under its nose to hitch it.

**The tanker:** fill or empty it with a bucket (or any fluid container) by right-clicking it. To move fuel in bulk,
park it with a tank beside it (within 2 blocks of its sides or ends) and right-click it with an empty hand: **Load**
takes from every tank, cauldron or machine nearby that will give up its fluid, and **Unload** fills everything
nearby that will take it, nearest first. So a tanker can fill up at a BuildCraft refinery's tanks and unload into a
row of pumps. Pumps only take their own kind of fuel, and never give any back, so you can't load from someone's pump.
A picked-up tanker keeps its load.

**The car hauler:** it carries cars and motorcycles up to a minivan's size, but not trucks, vans, buses or tractors.
Drive a car up to the hauler's back end, lined up with it, and press **G**: the car drives on
and stays on when you get out. With both spots full, nothing more goes on. Get in a car on the hauler and press **G**
to drive it off the back. Hitch the hauler to a semi and its cars go wherever it goes.

**Ownership and paint:** as with cars. **Sneak + right-click** with an empty hand locks or unlocks a trailer (its
owner, or anyone with its key), and a locked trailer can only be opened, hitched or loaded by its owner or someone
with its key. Right-click with a dye to
paint it. Punch it to pick it up. A box trailer spills its cargo.

## Campers and livestock trailers

Pickups and SUVs have a **tow ball**: the **Pickup Truck**, **Heavy-Duty Pickup**, **Electric Pickup**, **Off-Roader**,
**Minivan** and the **Farm Tractor**. They pull the two bumper-pull trailers, and the semi pulls bigger versions of
both on its fifth wheel:

| Trailer | Pulled by | Has |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Camper** | a tow ball | a double bed, 27 cupboard slots, a stove, an awning and two camp chairs |
| **Fifth-Wheel Camper** | the semi | a bedroom over the fifth wheel, 45 cupboard slots, a stove, a slide-out, an awning and two camp chairs |
| **Livestock Trailer** | a tow ball | pens for 6 cows, 3 horses or 12 chickens |
| **Semi Livestock Trailer** | the semi | pens for 12 cows, 6 horses or 24 chickens |

**Hitching to a tow ball:** put the trailer down, then back the pickup up to its tongue, so the ball is under the
coupler at the tongue's tip and the two are roughly in line, and press **G** (the dashboard shows **[G] Hitch
trailer**). Press G again to unhitch. A bumper-pull swings round behind on bends like the semi's trailers do, and can
swing up to 85° before it jackknifes. Pulling one, a pickup is a little slower and burns more fuel.

**Camping:** right-click a camper to go inside: its **cupboards** hold your things (a picked-up camper spills them),
its **stove** cooks food as a smoker does (anything a smoker cooks, with any fuel, but only while the camper's
parked: hitched up, the flame's out), and the **Sleep** button goes to bed. Sleeping in a camper works like a bed:
it sets your spawn to the ground outside its door, and once enough players are asleep the night passes. You can only
sleep in a parked camper, at night, with no monsters close by, and each camper sleeps two. You wake up outside the
door; moving or hitching the camper wakes everyone in it.

**Camp:** a camper standing unhitched for a moment sets up camp. Its **awning** rolls out with a string of **lights**
that glow at night (and so do its windows), its **steps** fold down under the door, its **stabilizer jacks** wind
down, and two **camp chairs**, a table and a cooler come out under the awning. Right-click a chair to sit in it.
The fifth-wheel camper's **slide-out** slides out too. Hitch it and it all folds away.

**Livestock:** cows, mooshrooms, sheep, pigs, goats, horses, donkeys, mules, llamas and chickens (the
`reikocars:livestock` entity tag, so a data pack can add more) ride in a livestock trailer, in pens down its length:
each pen holds one horse, two cows (or sheep, pigs, goats, llamas), or four chickens or young animals. Parked, the
trailer's back gate drops down as a **ramp**:

- Lead animals up the ramp on leads and they walk in, or right-click the trailer with them following you on leads to
  load them all. The leads come back to you.
- Right-click with an empty hand to let them all out down the ramp, or with a lead to take the last one in out on it.
- A hitched trailer opens its ramp while animals go in or out, but only once it's stopped.

Inside, they stand in their pens, seen between the slats, and go wherever the trailer goes. A full livestock trailer
counts as a load for the Trucker's pay; a camper doesn't.

**Building them:** as the other trailers, from the Trailer Chassis, the trailer's own body and four Tires (see
[Recipes](#recipes)).

## Building a car

Cars and trailers are put together at a **Car Workbench**. Each car has three parts of its own (a **chassis**, a
**body** and an **interior**), and they all take four **Tires** and an engine: a **Car Engine**, or a **Diesel
Engine** for the semi. A trailer takes the Trailer Chassis, its own body and four tires.

1. Craft the parts (see [Recipes](#recipes)) and place a Car Workbench.
2. Right-click the workbench. The buttons along the top are the cars it can build. Hover over one to see which parts
   you have, and click it to put all the parts you carry into place. You can also drop parts in by hand or shift-click
   them; a stack of tires fills all four wheels.
3. The slots are laid out like the car seen from above: a wheel at each corner, the engine at the front, the chassis
   in the middle with the body and interior either side. Empty slots show the part they need.
4. A 3D model of the car fills in as the parts go in: anything not fitted yet shows as a see-through blue ghost. Drag
   it to turn it.
5. When every part is in, take the car from the slot on the right. A green tick on a car's button means you carry
   everything for it.

Parts left in the workbench go back to you when you close it.

### With JEI

If JEI is installed, it gets a **Car Workbench** recipe page for each car and trailer: the parts laid out as in the
workbench, the vehicle turning in 3D, and a car's seats, top speed and tank size, or what a trailer carries. Look up a car's recipe (R), or what a part, tire or engine
is used for (U). Clicking the arrow in the workbench's screen lists every car. With the workbench open, JEI's **+**
button puts that car's parts in from your inventory, the same as clicking the car's button; if you're missing any,
the button is grayed out and JEI marks the missing parts in red.

## Gas and diesel pumps

There are two pumps, which work the same way:

- The **Gas Pump** (red, "GAS" on its sign) sells gasoline.
- The **Diesel Pump** (green, "DIESEL" on its sign) sells diesel: BuildCraft's Dense Fuel or Mixed Heavy Fuels, or
  other mods' diesel and biodiesel. It fills semis, and sells diesel by the bucket for BuildCraft's combustion
  engines and other mods' generators.

A pump is two blocks tall. Whoever places it owns it, and only they (or an admin) can break it. A broken pump keeps
its fuel and price on the item.

**Getting fuel in:** connect any pipe (BuildCraft's or another mod's) to either half of the pump, from any side.
The pump holds 16 buckets of its own kind of fuel, one fluid at a time. Nothing can be piped back out, so the only
way out is to sell it. The owner can also fill it from a bucket, and anyone can unload a tanker into it.

**Buying fuel:** right-click the pump. The screen shows the fuel in the pump and its price. A pump fills either the
car at the hose (within 6 blocks) or the **empty buckets** you carry. The little button at the start of the car row
switches between them, showing a car or a bucket. The screen starts on the car if one that runs on this fuel is at
the hose, and on buckets otherwise. Choose an amount (or **Fill up**), then pay:

- **Card:** the card you carry (the ⇄ button switches if you have several), its PIN, then **Card**. A debit card
  pays from its bank account; a credit card puts it on the bill.
- **Cash:** **Cash** pays with the coins and bills you carry, or from your wallet, and gives change.

This is the economy mod's own shop payment, so sales tax applies and the money goes straight into the owner's
bank account, even while they're offline. The pump's owner fills up for free, so they can also take fuel back out
of their own pump in buckets.

**Setting the price:** the owner sees a price row at the bottom of the screen. Type a price per bucket and press
**Set**. The lit display on the front of the pump shows the price, or EMPTY.

## Electric cars and charging stations

Five vehicles run on electricity: the **City EV**, the **Electric Sedan**, the **Electric Pickup**, the **Electric
Van** and the **Electric Moped** (see [The cars](#the-cars)). They drive like the rest, with a few differences:

- **The battery** is charged at a charging station, never with fuel; the dashboard shows it as a battery and a
  percentage, and a car's tooltip as "Battery". Out of charge, the dashboard says **BATTERY FLAT**.
- **No idling:** standing still with someone at the wheel uses nothing.
- **Regenerative braking:** braking puts some charge back (a stop from top speed gives back what 5 to 10 blocks of
  driving took), except with the wheels locked by the handbrake.
- **No oil** to change; the motor still wears like an engine, and a worn one throws sparks instead of smoke.
- **Quiet:** a soft chime instead of a starter, and a motor's whine that rises with speed instead of an engine.

**The Charging Station** is two blocks tall, like a pump; whoever places it owns it, and only they (or an admin) can
break it, which keeps its stored power and price on the item. It stores up to **500,000 MJ**, which comes in from
BuildCraft **engines** (any of them, redstone engines too) and **kinesis pipes** on either half, from any side, or,
as BuildCraft's config allows (it does by default, 10 FE to 1 MJ), from other mods' energy cables. So a few engines
can keep it topped up between customers. Nothing comes back out except into a car.

**Charging up:** park within 6 blocks of it (the same reach as the pumps' hose) and right-click it. The screen shows
the station's stored power, its price per 1,000 MJ, and your car's battery. Choose an amount (steps of 5,000 MJ, or
1,000 with shift held) or **Fill up**, then pay by card or cash, with sales tax, as at a pump. The money goes to the
owner, who charges for free. The owner sets the price per 1,000 MJ at the bottom of the screen; the display on the
front shows the price and how full the station is, or NO POWER.

A full charge costs much less than a tank of gasoline at the default prices (5 cents per 1,000 MJ: about $4 for the
City EV, $8 for the Electric Sedan).

## Wear and repairs

Cars wear out like real ones. Each car has five parts, each from 100% (new) down to 0%:

| Part | Wears from | When it's worn |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Oil** | distance: an oil change every 50,000 blocks or so | Below 25% the engine wears four times as fast, and twenty times as fast with none |
| **Engine** | distance, slowly (about 400,000 blocks), and hard crashes | Below 50%, less power: slower and slower to pick up speed. Any wear burns more fuel, up to half as much again |
| **Tires** | distance (80,000 blocks on a road), faster off-road and towing, and hard braking and handbrake skids | Below 30%, less grip: worse acceleration, braking and cornering |
| **Brakes** | every stop, by how fast you were going (about 550 hard stops) | Below 40%, the car takes longer to stop. Worn pads squeal |
| **Body** | crashes, by how hard you hit (a wall, another car or a mob) | Below 50%, slower. Bumps under about 7 mph leave no mark |

When any part reaches **0%, the car breaks down**: the engine won't start or run until it's repaired (the brakes
still work). Cars are never destroyed.

**Crash damage** shows where a car was hit. A head-on crash crumples the front, a side impact caves that side in, a
knock from behind dents the back, and the paint scrapes off around the dents. Harder hits **crack** the nearest glass
(the windshield, the back window or a side window), then **shatter** it, and smash the lamps at that corner. It isn't
only looks:

- **Smashed headlights** don't light the road at night; with one left, it's dimmer.
- A smashed lamp's **turn signal** stops blinking.
- A car bent much worse on one side than the other **pulls** toward that side as you drive.
- An engine worn below about a third **smokes** from under the hood, more the worse it gets.

Motorcycles have no glass, and their lamps break in pairs. Body Panels take the dents out as they mend the body; a
**Car Window** mends one cracked or shattered window (the shattered ones first) and a **Car Light** puts back one
smashed lamp. The Repair Bench shows the glass and the lamps as two more rows, and a car's tooltip says when they're
broken. Bumps too gentle to dent the body leave no mark.

**Trailers** wear too, though they have no oil or engine: their **tires** wear as they're towed (faster loaded or
off-road), their **brakes** whenever the semi brakes, and their **body** when the rig crashes (the load shoves the
trailer into the cab) or jackknifes (backing up until it folds against the cab). Worn trailer brakes and tires make
the whole rig slower to stop, and a battered trailer slows it down. A trailer with a part at 0% can't be towed: the
rig breaks down until you unhitch it (G), and it won't hitch again until it's fixed, so put a repair bench down
beside it (or pick it up and set it down by one).

**Electric cars** have no oil, so they wear on four parts: their motor counts as the engine, and a worn one throws
sparks instead of smoke. Their dashboards leave out the oil light.

**Seeing it:** the dashboard has five warning lights (oil can, engine, tire, brake, car). They come on amber when a
part is worn (25%), orange when badly worn (10%), and flash red when it has given out, with **BROKEN DOWN** on
the dashboard. Towing, a second row shows the trailer's tires, brakes and body. A tired engine smokes from under the hood, and the action bar tells the driver as each part gets
low. A car's or trailer's item tooltip shows its condition, and one picked up keeps its wear.

**The Repair Bench:** park within 6 blocks of it and right-click it. It works on any car or trailer there, locked
or not (a repair only helps its owner, and whoever orders it pays). With several beside the bench, the **< >**
arrows pick which; it starts on the one you're in, or else the nearest you can use. It shows the vehicle part by
part, with two ways to fix each:

- **Fit:** fits the parts you carry, for nothing. An oil change takes a Motor Oil, tires a full set (one per
  wheel; four for a trailer), brakes a set of Brake Pads (diesel trucks and trailers take two of each). Engine Parts
  each put back a quarter of the engine and Body Panels a fifth of the body (half that on diesel trucks and
  trailers), so you can fix them a bit at a time. A Car Window mends one broken window and a Car Light one smashed
  lamp.
- **The price button:** has the bench fit parts from its own stock, at the owner's prices. Click the jobs you want,
  then pay the total by **Card** (with PIN) or **Cash**, as at a pump. Sales tax applies, and the money goes to the
  bench's owner.

Whoever places the bench owns it, and only they (or an admin) can break it. The owner gets two more tabs: **Stock**,
to load the bench with parts (hoppers and pipes can fill it too, but nothing comes back out), and **Prices**, to set
the price of each part. The owner repairs from their own stock for free, so a bench works as a garage at home or a
mechanic's shop for other players.

## Dirt and car washes

Cars get dirty as they're driven: hardly at all on asphalt, more on other ground, much more off-road (grass, dirt,
sand, gravel, snow) and most in mud (and clay, farmland, podzol, soul sand), and twice as fast in the rain. A thousand
blocks or so off-road takes a clean car to filthy. The dirt shows: grime builds up from the sills and wheels, and the
windows get a dusty smear. Standing in the rain rinses a car off, but only so far (to 30%), and so does fording water
(to 15%).

- **Why wash it:** a dealership pays up to **15% less** for a filthy car, and a filthy car (75% dirty or worse)
  **rusts**: its body loses about 3% a Minecraft day until it's washed. A car's tooltip, its sale screen and the
  dealership's Trade in tab say how dirty it is, and a car keeps its dirt when it's picked up.
- **By hand:** right-click a car with a **bucket of water** (or any container of it) to throw it over the car. Each
  bucket takes a third of a filthy car's dirt off. A fire truck takes the water into its tank instead, until it's full;
  sneak to wash it.
- **The Car Wash** is a drive-through arch over four blocks of road: two blue posts and a CAR WASH beam, with two
  tall side brushes, one across the top and a spray bar. It's put down from its left-hand post (the one with the pay
  terminal), facing you, and reaches six blocks to your right and five up. Drive up to it (the dashboard shows
  **[G] Car wash**) and press **G** (or right-click it from the driver's seat) to pay its price by **Card** or
  **Cash**, plus sales tax, then **drive through slowly**: the brushes close in on the car's sides and come down on
  its roof, water rains from the spray bar, and a filthy car comes out clean after about a second under the arch.
  The wash is used up once the car's through. The owner's own cars wash free.
- **The Wash Bay** is a coin-op pressure washer. Right-click it and pay for a minute on it, and it hands you its
  lance (the **Pressure Washer**). Within **8 blocks** of the bay, hold right-click to spray: the jet washes whatever
  car it hits, a filthy one in about four seconds. The lance goes back on its holster once you leave. The bay's owner
  sprays free (sneak and right-click it for a lance).
- **Owners:** whoever puts a Car Wash or Wash Bay down owns it, and only they (or an admin) can break it; breaking
  any block of the arch takes it all down and gives it back. Right-click it on foot to set its price (0 is free) and
  see how many washes it's sold and what they've taken. New car washes charge $15.00 a wash, and wash bays $5.00 a
  minute (see [Settings](#settings)).

## Weather

Rain, snow and storms change how cars drive. With less grip a car is slower to get going, takes longer to stop, and
slides wide in hard turns:

| Weather | Grip |
| --- | --- |
| Dry | All of it |
| Rain (a wet road) | 80% |
| Thunderstorm | 65% |
| Falling snow, or snow on the road | 55% |
| Ice | As slippery as its block: ice, packed ice and blue ice are like glass |

- **Tires matter:** **Winter Tires** (a kit at the [Tuning Bench](#tuning)) win back most of the grip lost on snow
  (80%), bite into ice, and win back half of it in the rain. Off-road tires win back a third in snow; sport tires are
  worse in the wet. The dashboard shows the road beside the lock: **WET**, **STORM**, **SNOW** or **ICE**.
- **Wipers:** every car (not the motorcycles) has wipers on its windshield: two blades, or one in the middle of a
  narrow screen. They sweep while rain or snow falls on the car and its engine runs, faster in a storm. Raindrops (or
  snowflakes) gather on the glass while it's coming down, engine running or not, and the blades wipe away the ones
  they pass over; once it stops, they dry up. You see it all from the driver's seat, and so does anyone outside.
- **Spray:** water sprays up behind the wheels on a wet road, and snow on a snowy one, more the faster you go.
- **Snow on parked cars:** a car standing in falling snow is covered in about a minute and a half: its roof, hood,
  trunk and windshield (the glass stays clear while the engine runs). It blows off as the car's driven (in twenty
  blocks or so), melts in the rain or anywhere too warm to snow, and washes off at a car wash or with a bucket of
  water.
- **Thunderstorms:** besides the worse grip, lightning strikes a car out in a storm now and then (about six times an
  hour): a bolt, and 8% off its body. The people inside are safe, as in a real car, even from a bolt that strikes them
  there.
- **Headlights** come on by themselves in rain, snow and storms, lighting the road by day as well as at night.

## GPS

Press **N** (in a car or on foot) to open the GPS and pick somewhere to go:

- **Places:** the gas stations, charging stations, mechanics (Repair Benches), car washes, dealerships, car rentals,
  tuning shops, parking, DMVs, insurance offices, impound lots, race tracks and bus stops (with their routes) in this
  dimension, nearest first. A gas
  station's pumps are one place, with its cheapest gasoline and diesel a bucket (and **Out of fuel** if they're dry);
  a car wash shows its prices, and a charging station its price per 1,000 MJ. Type in the search box to narrow it
  down: *diesel*, *mechanic* or the owner's name. The GPS knows of a place once its area has been loaded since this
  version, however far away it is now.
- **My Cars:** the cars you own, and any you're renting, wherever you left them (even in another dimension, or far
  out of range). A car that's driven, towed or delivered somewhere else is followed there.
- **Waypoints** (with Xaero's Minimap): your waypoints in this dimension, and where you last died.
- **Coordinates:** type an X and a Z (and a Y, if you like), or **Use my spot** to remember where you are.

Once you've picked, a panel at the top of the screen shows where you're going, an **arrow** pointing the way (from
the way your car's facing, or you are, on foot), how far it is, how long at the speed you're going, and how far up or
down it is if that's more than a few blocks. You've arrived when you're within 12 blocks in a car or 4 on foot: a
chime, and the GPS turns off. **Stop** in the GPS turns it off before then. Distances are in feet and miles, or
meters and kilometers if your speedometer's in km/h.

**With Xaero's Minimap:** the destination is a waypoint (**GPS**) on the minimap, in the world and on Xaero's World
Map, until you get there; it isn't saved with your waypoints. Your cars show on the minimap and in the world too,
lettered by type (**S** for a sedan, **PT** for a pickup truck) in the nearest color to their paint. Either can be
turned off in `config/reikocars-client.toml`.

## Tow trucks

Two diesel tow trucks fetch cars that have broken down (or anything else that needs moving):

- **The flatbed** carries the car on its deck. Back up until the car's front or back is at the tail of the deck and
  press **G**: the car is winched up and rides on the deck facing forward. **G** again rolls it off behind.
- **The wrecker** lifts the car by its front wheels on its wheel-lift, the back wheels still on the road. Back the
  wheel-lift up to the car's front (the car facing the same way as the truck) and press **G**. The car swings out
  behind on bends, as a trailer does. **G** again lets it down where it is.

Either truck takes any car, van or pickup up to about 4.8 blocks long, broken down or not, as long as nobody is in
it and it's yours or unlocked. The flatbed carries motorcycles too; neither takes semis, buses, tractors or another
tow truck. (Cars left at an expired parking meter are the exception: a tow truck may take those, locked or not, to an
[impound lot](#impound-lots).) The dashboard shows **[G] Tow car** when there's one to pick up. A towed car doesn't run, burn fuel or
wear, and goes wherever the truck goes, saved with it. A loaded tow truck is a little slower and its tires and
brakes wear faster.

## Mail

**Mailboxes** send parcels between players. Whoever puts one up owns it (only they or an admin can break it), and
anyone can right-click it:

- **Send:** type who it's for, put up to nine stacks in the slots, and send. If the mailbox is the addressee's own,
  the parcel goes straight in, free. Otherwise you pay **postage** ($2.00 plus sales tax, by **Card** with PIN or
  **Cash**), the parcel waits in the mailbox for the mail van, and the mailbox's **flag goes up**. The addressee
  needs a mailbox of their own somewhere.
- **Inbox** (the owner's tab): the parcels that have come for you. Right-click a parcel to open it. Only its
  addressee can, or its sender if it comes back. A parcel's tooltip says who it's for, who it's from and where it's
  going. You get a chat message when mail arrives while you're on.

**The mail van** carries the mail. Drive up beside a mailbox (within 3 blocks) and press **G**: the van collects any
parcels waiting there into its mail compartment, and delivers the ones it's carrying for that mailbox's owner. The
driver **earns each parcel's postage** on delivery, so running a mail route pays. A parcel whose addressee no longer
has a mailbox goes back to the sender's mailbox instead. The van is right-hand drive, so the mailboxes are on the
driver's side.

## Work vehicles

- **Police car and ambulance:** the driver's **K** turns on the **light bar and siren**: the beacons flash red and
  blue (the ambulance's red and white) and the siren wails until **K** turns them off. In the dark the beacons light
  up the ground around the car too. The **ambulance** heals
  everyone riding in it except the driver, half a heart every two and a half seconds.
- **Garbage truck:** put **Trash Cans** out by the road (anyone can throw things in them; they hold 27 stacks). Back
  the truck's hopper up to them (within 3.5 blocks of its back end) and press **G**: every can in reach is emptied
  and the trash is crushed and gone. Each can with anything in it pays the **Garbage Collector** job, at most once a
  Minecraft day per can.
- **Ice cream truck:** a shop on wheels. Its owner (or anyone with its key) stocks the **freezer** (sneak +
  right-click) with whatever they like to sell, then right-clicks the **serving hatch** (on the truck's left side) to
  price each kind; clearing a price takes it off the menu. Everyone else right-clicks the truck to see the menu, picks
  a treat with the arrows and buys one at a time by **Card** with PIN or **Cash**, plus sales tax, paid to the owner.
  The horn (**H**) plays the ice cream jingle. **Vanilla, Chocolate and Strawberry Ice Cream** are quick snacks to
  stock it with.
- **Dump truck:** load the **bed** (sneak + right-click) and press **G**: the bed tips up and everything in it slides
  out behind the truck, then it comes back down.
- **Cement mixer:** fill the **drum** (sneak + right-click) with concrete powder. While the engine runs (someone at
  the wheel, with fuel), the drum turns and sets a block of powder into concrete every half second. **G** pours
  everything in the drum out of the chute at the back.
- **Fire truck:** see below.

## Fire trucks

The **Fire Truck** has a crew cab (two up front, two behind), red and white beacons and its own wailing siren
(**K**), equipment compartments (sneak + right-click, 18 slots), a **water tank** and an **aerial ladder**.

**The fire hose.** Hold a **Fire Hose** and keep right-click held within 16 blocks of a fire truck you may use (its
owner's, unlocked, or one you have the key to) that has water: a jet of water arcs out where you look, up to about
twenty blocks. You'll see the hose run from the truck's pump panel to your hand. The jet puts out fire, lit campfires
and candles where it lands and along the way, puts out burning mobs and players and shoves them back a little, and
stings water-shy mobs (endermen, blazes, striders, snow golems). It uses a fifth of a bucket a second, so a full tank
lasts about fifty seconds of spraying. The dashboard shows the water under the fuel gauge.

**Filling the tank**, three ways:

- **A fire hydrant:** park within 6 blocks of one (the pumps' hose length) and stop: it fills the truck at two
  buckets a second. A **Fire Hydrant** holds 8 buckets, piped in by BuildCraft's pipes (from a BuildCraft **pump**
  on a lake, say) or any mod's, or poured in by the bucket; pipes can't take it back out. Right-click it to see how
  much it has. A server can set `fireTruck.endlessHydrants` so hydrants never run dry, as if on the water mains.
- **Open water:** park beside (or over) a lake, river or pool, with still water within about four blocks of the middle
  of the truck, and its pump draws from it, a bucket a second. It doesn't use the water up.
- **By the bucket:** right-click the truck with a water bucket (or any container of water).

**The ladder.** Park and press **G**: the ladder swings up from the roof until it stands straight up behind the truck,
then slides out, up to 16 blocks from the ground or short of anything above. Back the truck up to a building and it
runs up the wall to the roof. Climb it like scaffolding: walk into it and hold **jump** to go up, **sneak** to hold
on, and step off at the top. **G** again brings it down. It needs 7 blocks of clear air behind the truck to go up,
the truck won't drive while it's up, and if the truck's moved anyway (pushed, towed) the ladder comes down. (The
climbable part is a column of invisible blocks behind the truck that tidy themselves away.)

**Firefighter** is a car job (see [Car jobs](#car-jobs)): every fire put out with the hose pays, except fires that
never go out (on netherrack, soul sand and the like), fires within 24 blocks of where the same player lit one with
flint and steel, a fire charge or lava in the last ten minutes, and more than 64 a day (`fireTruck.paidFiresPerDay`).

## Parking meters and tolls

**Parking meters** charge cars parked beside them. Whoever puts one up owns it (only they or an admin can break it).

- **Paying:** right-click the meter. It shows the car parked there (the nearest within **3 blocks**; a car between
  two meters belongs to the nearer one), how long it has left, and the rate. Pick the time with **-** and **+**, 10 to
  120 minutes at a go (four hours at most on the meter), and pay by **Card** with PIN or **Cash**, plus sales tax, to
  the meter's owner. Anyone can pay for any car.
- **Overstaying:** once a car's paid time runs out (or if none was bought) it gets **a minute's grace**. Then the
  meter's **red EXPIRED flag** goes up and the car's owner gets a **parking ticket**: the fine ($10.00) comes out of
  their bank account and goes to the meter's owner, with a chat message saying where. Another follows every **15
  minutes** the car stays unpaid. If they can't cover a fine, they pay what they have. A car with someone in it is
  waiting, not parked, and isn't ticketed.
- **The owner's side:** the owner's own cars park free, and they can add time free for anyone's car. The
  **Settings** tab sets the rate per 10 minutes and the fine, and shows what the meter has taken in parking and
  tickets.

**Toll gates** close the road until the toll is paid. A gate is a post with a striped barrier arm across the three
blocks to the right of whoever puts it down (facing them), so put it at the edge of the road. Closed, the arm's a
wall cars can't get past.

- **Paying a toll:** drive up to a closed gate; the dashboard shows **[G] Pay toll**. Press **G** and pay the toll by
  **Card** with PIN or **Cash**, plus sales tax. The arm swings up for a few seconds, and doesn't come down while
  anything's under it. The money goes to the gate's owner.
- **Toll Booths** set the toll: right-click your booth to set it (0 lifts the gate for free) and see how many cars
  have paid and what they've taken. A gate charges the toll of its owner's nearest booth within **12 blocks**; with
  no booth, it only opens for its owner. Anyone else right-clicking a booth is told the toll.
- **The owner** drives through free, and can right-click the gate to **hold it open** (and again to let it shut).
  Only the owner or an admin can break a gate or booth; breaking any block of a gate takes it all down and gives the
  gate back.

New meters charge $0.50 per 10 minutes with a $10.00 fine, and new booths a $2.00 toll (see [Settings](#settings)).

## Garages

A garage door is built from panels: **Garage Doors** (a house's sectional door, in white panels, rolling up into the
garage) or **Roller Shutters** (metal slats that coil up into a box over the opening). Fill a doorway with them and,
as soon as they make a full rectangle, they join into **one door**, up to **16 wide and 10 high** (see
[Settings](#settings)). Until then they're loose panels; the action bar says how far there is to go. Panels of
different kinds, facing different ways or put up by different players don't join.

- **Opening it:** right-click it. A door opens for its **owner** (whoever put its panels up), an admin, and anyone
  carrying a **Garage Remote** linked to it; from **inside**, anyone can open it, so nobody's shut in. It takes a
  second or two to go up or down, clears the doorway a row at a time, and backs up rather than come down on anyone or
  anything in the way.
- **Driving up:** a door **opens by itself** for a car coming at it (on either side, within 8 blocks) if its driver
  may open it or it's the owner's car, and shuts again 3 seconds after the car's through. In front of a door, the
  dashboard shows **[G] Open/close garage door** for the key too.
- **Remotes:** sneak-right-click your door with a remote to link it (up to **4 doors** a remote; again to unlink),
  then give it to whoever you want to let in. Right-click with it, anywhere, even driving, to open or close the
  nearest of its doors within **64 blocks**. **Reset remotes** on the door's screen gives the door a new code, and
  every remote linked to it stops working.
- **Buttons:** a **Garage Door Button** on a wall works the nearest door within **16 blocks** (sneak-right-click it
  to look again). Inside the garage it's for anyone; outside, only for whoever may open the door. No redstone.
- **Its screen:** sneak-right-click the door with an empty hand. Its **name** (shown on remotes and the GPS),
  **windows** (a row along the top of a sectional door), whether it **opens for cars**, and which side's the garage
  (worked out from the roof when it's built: **Swap sides** if it guessed wrong).
- **Paint:** right-click it with any dye. Only its owner (or an admin) can paint it, set it up or break it.

A sectional door rolls up its track and, where there's room above the opening inside, lies flat under the ceiling on
its rails, with an opener; with no room there it slides up into the wall.

**Safe in the garage:** a car behind a **closed** garage door (within 16 blocks of it, across its width) and under a
**roof** can't be got into, towed away or impounded by anyone but its owner and whoever has its key, locked or not. It
doesn't rust while it's in there, and no snow settles on it.

**Parking garages:** turn on **Parking garage** on the door's screen and set its prices for an **hour** and a
**day** (a Minecraft hour is 50 seconds; new garages charge $1.00 and $10.00). A driver who pulls up to it (and couldn't
open it anyway) sees **[G] Pay to park**: press **G** and pay for 1, 2, 6 or 12 hours or 1, 2, 3 or 7 days, by
**Card** with PIN or **Cash**, plus sales tax, to the owner. The door then opens for that car, coming and going,
until its time's up. A car still inside after that sees **[G] Pay to leave**: the hours it's overstayed, to get out.
The owner's cars (and anyone with a remote) park free. Parking garages are on the GPS, with their hourly price, and the
door's screen shows how many cars are parked, the visits and the takings.

## Insurance

**Insurance Offices** sell car insurance. Whoever puts one up owns it (only they or an admin can break it): they're
the insurer, and they pay the claims from their bank account.

- **Buying cover:** park your car within **10 blocks** of an office and right-click the desk. Pick the car with the
  arrows (if you've parked more than one), and pay the premium ($10.00 for **7 Minecraft days** of cover, plus sales
  tax, by **Card** with PIN or **Cash**) to the office's owner. Buying again at the same office adds another term, up
  to four ahead. Buying at another office replaces the old cover. A car's tooltip shows who insures it and how long
  is left, and cover goes with the car when it's picked up.
- **Claims:** insurance covers **crash damage**. When an insured car's **body, glass or lamp repairs** are paid for
  at someone's Repair Bench, the insurer pays back what they cost over the **deductible** ($10.00), up to the **claim limit**
  ($100.00 a claim), straight to whoever paid the bill. The bench's screen shows what'll come back. Wear (oil,
  engine, tires and brakes) isn't covered, and an insurer who can't afford a claim doesn't pay it.
- **Lapsing:** cover runs out at the end of its term, and stops if the car changes hands.
- **The owner's side:** the owner's screen sets the premium, deductible and claim limit, and shows the books:
  policies sold and claims paid. The owner can't insure their own cars at their own office.

The workbench recipes are data files (`data/reikocars/recipe/sedan.json` and so on, type `reikocars:car_assembly`),
so a data pack can change a car's parts or add new vehicles. Each one names a `chassis`, `body`, `interior`, `engine`

recipes do, and then those slots must be empty. `"wheels": 2` makes it a two-wheeler, as the motorcycles are.

## Building and testing

You need JDK 21. The two mods it builds on are in `libs/`: replace those jars to build against newer versions
(and update the names in `gradle.properties`). JEI comes from its Maven repository: the build compiles against
the API of `jei_version` in `gradle.properties` (19.21.2.313, so the plugin works with that and every newer 1.21.1
JEI) and puts JEI in the dev runs. `-Pjei_version=...` tries another version.

Xaero's Minimap isn't ours to share, so the GPS compiles against stand-ins for the few of its classes it uses
(`src/xaeroStubs/`), which never go in the jar. To try it in a dev client, put Xaero's Minimap's jar (and World
Map's, if you like) in a folder and pass it: `./gradlew runClient -PxaeroJars=/path/to/folder`.

Patchouli comes from its Maven repository too: the build compiles against its API and puts it in the dev runs. The
Car Manual's pages are written by `tools/guidebook/book.py` (`python3 tools/guidebook/book.py`), from its own text,
the workbench recipes and the car table above; edit that and run it rather than the book's JSON. Its pictures are
cropped from showcase screenshots by `tools/guidebook/pictures.py` (it needs Pillow).

`-PnoOptionalMods` leaves JEI and Patchouli out of a dev run, to check the mod works without them (the Car Manual's
game test then checks there's no manual and no recipe for one).

./gradlew runClient -Pshowcase=diesel     # the same for the gas and diesel pumps and selling buckets

## Editing the models

The cars are Blockbench projects in `blockbench/` (format: Modded Entity, box UV), built at Minecraft's own scale:
one texture pixel to one model pixel. They have real wheel arches with dark wells, tires with recessed rims and a
hub, raked windshields and rear windows, furnished cabins (seats, dashboards with lit gauges, steering wheels, door
panels), and detailed lights, grilles, bumpers, mirrors and trim. Each has the same top-level bones, which the mod
relies on:

- `body`: the paintwork. Paint it light gray: the game tints it the car's color.
- `trim`: glass (may be see-through), seats, bumpers, grille and so on, drawn as painted.
- `lights`: head- and taillights, which glow while the engine runs.
- `wheel_fl`, `wheel_fr`, `wheel_rl`, `wheel_rr`: each pivoting on its axle. They roll, and the front pair steers.
- `wheel_rl2`, `wheel_rr2` (the semi only): the second rear axle, which rolls with the first.
- `signal_l`, `signal_r`: amber copies of each side's turn lamps, a shade bigger than the lamps they cover, drawn
  glowing only while that side blinks. Trailers have them too, over their tail and marker lights.

The work vehicles have a few optional bones as well: `beacon_a` and `beacon_b` (emergency lights that flash in turn),
`bed` (the dump truck's, pivoting at its hinge at the back) and `drum_mount` holding `drum` (the mixer's, turning about
its own length).

The tow trucks' decks and wheel-lift are part of `trim`, so a towed car must sit where the code puts it: the
flatbed's deck top 20 pixels up, running from 4 pixels ahead of the middle to 59 behind, and the wrecker's forks 61
pixels behind the middle (see `tow/Towing.java`). The wrecker's boom is a child bone tilted up from its pivot.

A motorcycle has `body`, `trim`, `lights`, `wheel_rl` (its back wheel) and a `fork`, which steers and holds the
forks, handlebars and `wheel_fl` (its front wheel) inside it. It has no right-hand wheels.

The trailers (`box_trailer`, `tanker_trailer`, `car_hauler`) work the same way, with `body`, `trim`, `lights` and
two axles of wheels, `wheel_l1`, `wheel_r1`, `wheel_l2` and `wheel_r2`. Their textures are in
`textures/entity/trailer/`. The front faces north, and the kingpin (which sits on the semi's fifth wheel) must stay
56 pixels ahead of the middle and 18 up, as set in `trailer/TrailerType.java`.

These bones can hold child bones of their own, turned at an angle: the windshields, rear windows and pillars, the
steering wheels, a motorcycle's raked fork legs (`fork_legs`, inside `fork`, so they steer with it), the semi's roof
fairing, the tanker's tank. A child is drawn the way its parent is (tinted under `body`, lit under `lights`). Some
textures aren't square (512×256, say); Blockbench and the game both take that.

The car's front faces north (−Z), and the driver's side is **−X** in Blockbench. To change a car, edit its
`.bbmodel`, save the texture over `src/main/resources/assets/reikocars/textures/entity/car/<car>.png`, use
**File > Export > Export Java Entity**, and paste the exported `createBodyLayer()` into
`client/model/<Car>Model.java`. If you move the seats, update the seat table at the top of `car/CarType.java`.

The pumps, the Car Workbench, the Repair Bench and the mailbox are block models: `models/block/gas_pump.json` (the
diesel pump uses the same model with `textures/block/diesel_pump.png`), `models/block/car_workbench.json`,
`models/block/repair_bench.json` and `models/block/mailbox.json` (with `mailbox_flag_up.json` for when there's mail
waiting to go out), each with one 128×128 texture. The
pump's model is two blocks tall and faces north. Its screen's face must stay at z = 3, 19 to 26 pixels up, and its
sign's face at z = 3, 28 to 32 up, because that's where the lit price and the fuel's name are drawn. The screen and
sign glow (`neoforge_data` light on those elements).

The Car Wash is a block model per part of its arch, all sharing `textures/block/car_wash.png`: `car_wash_base` (the
left post's foot, with the pay terminal), `car_wash_base_far`, `car_wash_post`, `car_wash_beam_end` and
`car_wash_beam_1` to `car_wash_beam_4`, which spell CAR WASH two letters a block (backwards from behind, so it reads
from both sides). Its brushes are drawn in code (`client/CarWashRenderer.java`), and the dirt and snow on cars are
made from each car's own texture (`client/CarGrime.java`). The wipers find each car's windshield in its model (the
biggest forward-facing pane of see-through glass in `trim`, with any panes in line with it: `client/Wipers.java`), so a
new car gets them as long as its windshield is glass that faces forward.
